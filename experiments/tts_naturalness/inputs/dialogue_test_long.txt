You dont understand, she said, leaning forward across the table. This isnt about the money. It never was. Its about what he did. What he got away with.

I stirred my coffee. The steam curled up between us, blurring her face for a second. What are you going to do about it?

Nothing, she said, and smiled. It was not a nice smile. Nothing at all. Not yet. But he doesnt know that. Thats the best part. He thinks Im going to come after him. He spends every single night lying awake, wondering when its going to happen. And thats going to go on for the rest of his life.

I nodded. I could see it. The slow, quiet revenge. The kind that doesnt make the papers. The kind that works.

Youre a cold woman, I said.

No, she said. Im just patient.

The waitress came over and filled our cups again. She didnt say anything, just poured and walked away. My companion watched her go, then leaned back in her chair.

You want to know the worst part? she asked.

I waited.

Hes already won. Three years ago, when he did what he did, he already won. The money, the reputation, all of it, he already got away with it. What Im doing now, its not about winning anymore. Its about making sure he knows. Every single day for the rest of his life, hes going to know that somebody out there knows what he did. Somebody who remembers. Somebody who wont forget.

She picked up her cup and took a sip. Her hand was steady. There was no tremor, no hesitation. She had been waiting a long time for this.

What do you want me to do? I asked.

Nothing. Youre just here for the coffee. She smiled again, and this time it was different. This time it reached her eyes. Just drink your coffee and dont worry about me. This isnt your fight. This is just something I need to do.

And then she was quiet for a long moment, looking out the window at the street, at the people passing by, at the ordinary afternoon happening outside while we sat inside with our cold coffee and our warm silence.

You know what the funny thing is? she said finally. I dont even hate him anymore. I stopped hating him a long time ago. This isnt about hate. Its about justice. The kind that doesnt fit in a courtroom. The kind that doesnt have a verdict. The kind that just is.

She finished her coffee and set down the cup carefully, precisely, as if placing it in exactly the right spot.

The check came. She reached for it, but I was faster.

This ones mine, I said.

Next one is yours, she agreed.

We walked out together into the afternoon. She turned left, I turned right. And that was the last time I ever saw her.
